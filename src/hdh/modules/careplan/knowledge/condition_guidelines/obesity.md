CONCERN — Obesity contributes to several other conditions on most problem
lists it appears on, which makes it easy to treat as background rather than as
something a plan addresses. It is also the concern most likely to be written in
language the patient experiences as blame, so how it is phrased matters more
here than almost anywhere else.

GOAL — A weight change the patient has chosen and considers achievable,
expressed as a modest percentage over a stated period rather than an ideal
figure. For many patients the more useful goal is a functional or metabolic
one — a target the weight change serves — rather than the number itself.

INTERVENTIONS — Ask what the patient wants to change before proposing anything,
since a plan they did not choose will not be followed. Review medications that
promote weight gain and ask whether alternatives exist. Offer structured
support rather than advice alone. Where other conditions are present, make the
link explicit — the connection between weight and the symptom the patient
actually minds is usually more motivating than the weight itself. Owners:
practice nurse or dietitian for structured support, prescribing clinician for
medication review.

REVIEW — At the interval agreed with the patient, and alongside review of the
conditions the weight change is intended to help.
