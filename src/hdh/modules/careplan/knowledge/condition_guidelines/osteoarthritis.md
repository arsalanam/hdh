CONCERN — Osteoarthritis is a condition of function rather than of numbers:
what matters is what the patient can no longer do, and what they are taking in
order to keep doing it. The analgesic side of that is where a care plan most
often finds risk, particularly where anti-inflammatories meet renal impairment,
heart failure, or an anticoagulant.

GOAL — Pain controlled well enough for the activities the patient names as
important, on analgesia whose risks are acceptable for their other conditions.
Stating the activity rather than a pain score is what makes this goal
meaningful and checkable.

INTERVENTIONS — Review analgesia against the patient's other conditions, since
the commonest harm in this condition comes from its treatment rather than from
the arthritis. Where an anti-inflammatory is in use, ask specifically whether
it remains necessary and whether anything else in the regimen makes it
dangerous. Offer exercise and weight management as first-line rather than
adjunct, since both change the condition and neither carries drug risk.
Consider referral where function is deteriorating. Owners: prescribing
clinician for analgesia, physiotherapy for function.

REVIEW — At an interval matched to how fast function is changing, and whenever
analgesia is escalated.
