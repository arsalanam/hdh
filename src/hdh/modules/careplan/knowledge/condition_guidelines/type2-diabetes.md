---
CONCERN — Type 2 diabetes that is not meeting its agreed target exposes the
patient to microvascular damage over years and, where treatment is intensive,
to hypoglycaemia over hours. Both directions matter, and which one dominates
depends on the person: a working adult with decades of exposure ahead is a
different problem from an older adult in whom tight control buys little and
costs a great deal. A concern written for this condition should say which of
those two risks is being managed.

GOAL — A glycaemic target the patient and clinician have agreed and written
down, with the reasoning attached. Typical framings are an HbA1c band rather
than a single figure, reviewed at three to six months. Where deintensification
is the intent, the target is stated as a floor as well as a ceiling, so that a
rising number is not misread as a lapse.

INTERVENTIONS — Review the regimen against the agreed target; agents carrying
hypoglycaemia risk deserve particular scrutiny where the target has been
relaxed. Confirm the patient can carry out what is being asked, including
monitoring frequency and any injection technique. Where self-monitoring is
expected, agree what the readings will be used for, since readings nobody acts
on are a burden without a benefit. Owners: prescribing clinician for agent
changes, diabetes nurse or pharmacist for technique and education.

REVIEW — HbA1c at three to six months, depending on stability and on whether
the regimen has just changed. Renal function and foot examination on the annual
cycle, and sooner where an agent's dosing depends on clearance.
