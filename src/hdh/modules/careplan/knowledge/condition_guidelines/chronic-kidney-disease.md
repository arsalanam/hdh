CONCERN — Reduced kidney function changes two things at once: the trajectory
of the kidney disease itself, and the safety of everything else the patient
takes. The second is usually the more immediate concern in a care plan, because
a dose appropriate at normal clearance may accumulate to harm at stage 3 or
beyond. Any plan for a patient with chronic kidney disease should say
explicitly which of their medications its reduced clearance affects.

GOAL — Stable renal function, expressed as a rate of decline rather than a
single figure, alongside a medication list that has been checked against
current clearance. Blood-pressure and glycaemic targets are usually part of
this goal rather than separate from it.

INTERVENTIONS — Review every agent whose dosing or safety depends on renal
clearance, and adjust or stop where indicated. Identify agents that are
directly nephrotoxic and ask whether they remain necessary. Confirm the patient
knows to seek advice during intercurrent illness, when dehydration can move
clearance sharply. Where progression is a concern, agree in advance what would
trigger a referral. Owners: prescribing clinician for dose review, pharmacist
for the clearance and interaction check.

REVIEW — Renal function and electrolytes at an interval set by stage and
stability, and after any change to an agent that affects them. Sooner during
acute illness.
