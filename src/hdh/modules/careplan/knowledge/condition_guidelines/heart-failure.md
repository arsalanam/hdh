CONCERN — Heart failure is a condition of decompensation: the patient is
usually stable until they are suddenly not, and the interval between the first
extra kilogram and the hospital admission is where a care plan can act. Fluid
status, adherence and intercurrent illness are the common triggers, and each is
something a plan can name in advance rather than discover afterwards.

GOAL — The patient remains free of admission for decompensation, with symptoms
and weight stable at a level they and the clinician have agreed as their
baseline. Where full symptom control is not achievable, the goal is better
stated as the level of activity the patient wants to retain.

INTERVENTIONS — Agree a daily or near-daily weight, a threshold that prompts
contact, and who to contact; a plan that says "monitor weight" without those
three parts is not actionable. Review agents that worsen fluid retention or
renal function. Confirm the patient can tell which symptoms mean urgent review
rather than routine. Where the patient lives alone, name who else is positioned
to notice deterioration. Owners: prescribing clinician for the regimen,
heart-failure or community nurse for monitoring and the escalation route.

REVIEW — Renal function and electrolytes after any diuretic or
renin-angiotensin agent change. Symptom and weight review at an interval
matched to stability.
