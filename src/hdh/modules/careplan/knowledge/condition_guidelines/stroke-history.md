CONCERN — A previous stroke or transient ischaemic attack is the strongest
single predictor of the next one, which makes secondary prevention the whole of
the concern. The recorded history is also easy to overlook precisely because it
sits in the past tense while the risk it represents is entirely present.

GOAL — Secondary-prevention measures in place and tolerated, with blood
pressure, lipids and any anticoagulation decision at their agreed targets, and
no recurrent event over the review period. Where residual impairment is
present, the goal usually includes maintaining function and independence rather
than risk figures alone.

INTERVENTIONS — Confirm that antithrombotic or anticoagulant therapy matches
the mechanism of the original event, since the right choice differs between
them. Review blood-pressure control, which contributes more to recurrence risk
than any other modifiable factor. Ask about swallowing, mobility, mood and
cognition, each of which changes what the rest of the plan can realistically
ask. Address driving and safety where relevant. Owners: prescribing clinician
for the regimen, therapy and community services for function.

REVIEW — Annually as a minimum, and after any new neurological symptom, however
brief, since brevity is not reassurance in this condition.
