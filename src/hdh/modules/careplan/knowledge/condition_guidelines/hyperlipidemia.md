{"conditions": ["hyperlipidemia"], "icd10": ["E78.5"], "topic": "lipid management"}
---
CONCERN — Raised lipids are a modifiable contributor to vascular events and,
like blood pressure, produce no symptoms to prompt attention. The decision to
treat rests on overall cardiovascular risk rather than on the lipid figure
alone, which means the same result justifies different action in different
patients. Where vascular disease is already established, the question is not
whether to treat but whether current treatment is adequate and tolerated.

GOAL — A lipid profile at or below the agreed threshold, on treatment the
patient is actually taking and tolerating. Where a statin has previously been
stopped for side effects, a realistic goal may be tolerance of any effective
agent rather than a particular number.

INTERVENTIONS — Confirm what the patient is currently taking and at what dose,
since lipid-lowering therapy is among the most commonly stopped without anyone
recording it. Ask directly about muscle symptoms rather than waiting for them
to be volunteered. Review interactions where other agents are present. Discuss
diet and activity as adjuncts, framed as achievable change rather than an
instruction. Owners: prescribing clinician for agent and dose, pharmacist for
interaction and tolerance review.

REVIEW — A lipid profile eight to twelve weeks after starting or changing
therapy, then annually once stable.
