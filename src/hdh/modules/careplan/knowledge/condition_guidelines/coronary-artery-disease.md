---
CONCERN — Established coronary disease moves the patient from primary to
secondary prevention, where the argument for treatment is stronger and the
tolerance for gaps is lower. The concern is usually less about the diagnosis
than about whether secondary-prevention therapy is complete, current and
actually being taken.

GOAL — Secondary-prevention therapy in place and tolerated, symptoms stable at
a level the patient accepts, and modifiable risk factors at their agreed
targets. Where symptoms limit activity, the goal is best expressed as the
activity the patient wants to regain.

INTERVENTIONS — Reconcile the current regimen against what secondary prevention
would ordinarily include, and record a reason wherever something is absent —
an unexplained gap and a deliberate omission look identical in a record six
months later. Ask about symptom pattern and what provokes it. Address blood
pressure, lipids, glycaemia and smoking as one combined risk conversation
rather than four separate ones. Owners: prescribing clinician for the regimen,
practice nurse for risk-factor review and lifestyle support.

REVIEW — Annually where stable, and promptly on any change in symptom pattern,
which is the change that matters most in this condition.
