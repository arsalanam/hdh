CONCERN — Atrial fibrillation matters chiefly because of stroke risk, and
that risk is manageable with anticoagulation whose own principal harm is
bleeding. A care plan for this condition is therefore almost always about
holding those two against each other for this particular patient, and saying
where the balance was struck.

GOAL — An anticoagulation decision that is current, documented with its
reasoning, and understood by the patient, together with a rate or rhythm
strategy achieving symptom control. Where anticoagulation has been declined or
withheld, the goal includes recording why, so the decision can be revisited
rather than silently inherited.

INTERVENTIONS — Review the anticoagulation decision against present-day risk
rather than the risk at the time it was made, since both sides of the balance
change with age and renal function. Check for agents that increase bleeding
risk alongside an anticoagulant. Confirm the patient knows what to do about a
missed dose and what bleeding warrants urgent review. Where a vitamin K
antagonist is used, confirm the monitoring arrangement is actually working.
Owners: prescribing clinician for the anticoagulation decision, pharmacist for
interaction review and adherence.

REVIEW — Annually as a minimum, and after any change in renal function,
bleeding event, or addition of an agent affecting bleeding risk.
