CONCERN — Iron deficiency anaemia is a finding rather than a diagnosis, and
the concern it should raise in a care plan is twofold: correcting the
deficiency, and establishing why it happened. Treating the number without
answering the second question is the common failure, and in adults the
unanswered question is sometimes serious.

GOAL — Haemoglobin and iron stores restored to the agreed range, with a cause
identified or actively being investigated, and treatment the patient tolerates
well enough to complete.

INTERVENTIONS — Establish the likely source of loss or the reason for poor
absorption, and record what has been considered and excluded. Review
medications that contribute to gastrointestinal blood loss, particularly
anti-inflammatories and antithrombotics. Discuss how iron is taken, since
tolerance is the main reason courses are abandoned and alternate-day dosing is
often better tolerated. Owners: prescribing clinician for investigation and
replacement, pharmacist for tolerance and administration.

REVIEW — Haemoglobin and iron stores after the agreed treatment interval, and
again some months after stopping, since recurrence points back at an
unaddressed cause.
