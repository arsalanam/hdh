CONCERN — Hypothyroidism on replacement therapy is usually straightforward,
and the concern is generally one of two things: replacement that has drifted
out of range, or symptoms attributed to the thyroid that the thyroid does not
explain. Over-replacement carries its own risks, particularly in older adults
and in atrial fibrillation, and is the direction most often missed.

GOAL — Thyroid function within the target range on a stable dose, with symptoms
either resolved or attributed elsewhere. In older adults, or where cardiac
disease is present, the target may deliberately sit at the less-suppressed end.

INTERVENTIONS — Confirm how the dose is actually taken, since absorption is
affected by timing and by several common co-prescriptions. Review interacting
medications and supplements. Where symptoms persist despite biochemical
control, avoid escalating the dose to treat them and look elsewhere for the
cause. Owners: prescribing clinician for dose, pharmacist for interaction and
administration review.

REVIEW — Thyroid function six to eight weeks after any dose change, then
annually once stable.
