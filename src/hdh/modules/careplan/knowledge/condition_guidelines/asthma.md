CONCERN — Asthma that appears mild between attacks can still be dangerous
during one, and reliever overuse is the signal that most often precedes a
serious event. The concern for a care plan is usually control rather than
diagnosis: how often symptoms interrupt sleep or activity, and how much
reliever is actually being used.

GOAL — Symptoms and reliever use within the agreed threshold, with no night
waking attributable to asthma and no unscheduled care over the review period.
Stating the reliever threshold explicitly is what makes this goal measurable.

INTERVENTIONS — Check inhaler technique and adherence to preventer therapy
before escalating anything, since the commonest cause of poor control is a
preventer not being taken. Agree a written action plan covering deterioration.
Identify and address triggers where they are modifiable. Review any agent that
can provoke bronchospasm. Owners: practice or respiratory nurse for technique,
adherence and the action plan, prescribing clinician for regimen changes.

REVIEW — At least annually, and after any course of oral steroid or any
unscheduled attendance, both of which are markers that control has slipped.
