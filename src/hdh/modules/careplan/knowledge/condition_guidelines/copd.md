CONCERN — In COPD the events that matter are exacerbations: each one costs
lung function that does not fully return, and the strongest predictor of the
next is the last. A plan built around the stable state misses the point, so the
concern is usually best framed around exacerbation frequency and what happens
when one begins.

GOAL — Fewer exacerbations than the preceding year, and none that reach
hospital because help was sought too late. Where breathlessness is the dominant
complaint, the goal is stated as a level of activity the patient wants back
rather than a lung-function number.

INTERVENTIONS — Confirm inhaler technique directly rather than assuming it,
because a substantial share of apparent treatment failure is delivery failure.
Agree a written action plan naming the symptoms that mean start treatment and
the symptoms that mean seek help the same day. Address smoking where it is
present, as the single intervention that changes the trajectory. Check
vaccination status. Review agents that may worsen breathlessness. Owners:
respiratory or practice nurse for technique and the action plan, prescribing
clinician for the regimen.

REVIEW — After every exacerbation, since that is when the plan has just been
tested. Otherwise annually, or sooner where treatment has changed.
