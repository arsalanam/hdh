CONCERN — Blood pressure above the agreed target is a risk carried silently:
the patient feels nothing, and the harm accrues in the vessels of the brain,
heart and kidneys. That silence is itself the management problem, since nothing
about the condition prompts anyone to act. Where other vascular conditions are
already present, the same number carries more weight.

GOAL — A blood pressure within the agreed range on readings the patient and
clinician both trust, sustained across at least two visits. Targets are
individualised — age, frailty, renal function and postural symptoms all move
them — so the plan should record the number chosen rather than implying a
universal one. In older or frail patients, the risk of standing hypotension may
set the floor.

INTERVENTIONS — Confirm the readings before changing treatment: technique, cuff
size and white-coat effect account for a substantial share of apparent poor
control, and home or ambulatory readings often settle the question. Review
adherence before adding an agent, since a drug not taken looks identical to a
drug not working. Where several agents are already in use, check for a postural
drop before intensifying. Owners: prescribing clinician for agent changes,
practice nurse for measurement technique and home readings.

REVIEW — Repeat readings within four to twelve weeks of any change, extending
to six or twelve months once stable. Renal function and electrolytes after
starting or increasing an agent that affects them.
