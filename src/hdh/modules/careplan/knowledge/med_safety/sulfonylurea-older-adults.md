Sulfonylureas lower glucose whether or not the patient has eaten, so the
risk they carry is hypoglycaemia rather than treatment failure. In older
adults that risk rises: renal clearance falls with age, meals become less
regular, and the adrenergic warning symptoms that would normally prompt
someone to eat are blunted or absent.

Severity depends heavily on whether help is nearby. A hypoglycaemic
episode that someone else notices is usually an inconvenience; the same
episode alone, at night, is the one that ends in a fall, an ambulance, or
worse. Living alone is therefore part of the drug's risk profile, not a
separate social note.

Where a sulfonylurea is already prescribed to an older adult, the usual
questions are whether the glycaemic target still justifies it, whether an
agent with less hypoglycaemia risk would do, and whether the patient can
recognise and treat a low without assistance.
