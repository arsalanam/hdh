A glycaemic target that suits a healthy adult in their forties is often
the wrong target for someone in their eighties. The benefit of tight
control accrues over many years, while the harm of hypoglycaemia is
immediate — so as remaining life expectancy shortens and comorbidity
accumulates, the balance moves.

Deintensification is the usual name for acting on that: relaxing the
target, and reducing or stopping the agents that were driving it. It is an
active clinical decision rather than neglect, and it is worth recording as
one, because a relaxed target with no rationale reads later like a lapse.

Renal impairment shifts the same balance further, since several glucose-
lowering agents accumulate as clearance falls.
