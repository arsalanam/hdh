NSAIDs constrict the afferent arteriole by suppressing prostaglandin
synthesis. In a healthy kidney that is tolerated; in chronic kidney disease
the same constriction removes a compensation the kidney was relying on, and
filtration falls further.

The same prostaglandin effect causes sodium and water retention, which is
the mechanism by which an NSAID destabilises heart failure. A patient with
both conditions is exposed twice over, and the two failures reinforce each
other — worsening renal function limits diuresis, and fluid overload
worsens renal perfusion.

The risk is not evenly distributed across the class or the dose. It rises
with duration, with dose, and sharply when an ACE inhibitor or ARB and a
diuretic are also on the list, since all three act on the same regulatory
pathway.

Where an NSAID appears alongside CKD stage 3 or worse, or heart failure,
the usual questions are what symptom it is treating, whether that symptom
has a non-NSAID answer, and — if it continues — how renal function and
fluid status will be watched.
